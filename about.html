About

Garrett Summers

Keyboards, synthesizers & organs — Willy on the Wire, keys for Rob Wolf. Based in Bozeman, Montana.

Early Foundations

I started playing piano when I was two, by ear, and started formal classical lessons at four. I picked up trumpet in fifth grade and started playing jazz on it in sixth grade — but my teacher, Caleb Scarguff, realized I was better on piano and moved me over. I loved it so much I dropped the classical lessons entirely and started taking jazz lessons instead.

First Bands

I joined my first rock band at 12, the summer before seventh grade, through a program called The Lesson Studio in Boulder, Colorado — they put a handful of us kids together with an adult musician who taught us how to actually function as a band. At 15 I started my first real band, a punk outfit called Break Free, with Ray Belmont, Sam Usinski, and Kate Eubanks. I didn't have a car, so my friends would come pick me up and haul my keyboard to Ray's house to practice. That band ran about 18 months. Ray's gone on to do some great things since.

I moved to Montana after high school with my piano in tow, not thinking music was going to be a real part of my life out here.

Retrograde

That changed at my own birthday party sophomore year of college. I ran into Gus and Jack in my basement during a party of about a hundred people, we started jamming, and there was an immediate connection. That became Retrograde.

We wrote songs, made a video for Battle of the Bands, and it caught the attention of two MSU students, Davis Rael and Alex Johnson, who offered to record our debut album even though they'd never produced one before. We cleared out the basement of their farmhouse on Bozeman Trail Road and got to work. My best friend Eli Manfure flew out for spring break and we locked ourselves in for two weeks straight, then kept going at five to six hours a day, six days a week, into May. We played our first show as Retrograde headlining in that same yard to about 500 people.

I spent that summer interning at MKS Instruments in Boulder during the day and mixing the album every night — didn't leave the house much. We released our debut album, Speed of Sound, on August 15, 2025, and things took off fast: we opened for Pegas and The Rooftops at MSU Debut, started playing campus shows weekly, and got a float in the Gold Rush Parade where we played our single "Kerosene" in front of a big crowd.

I left the band shortly after that stretch, following the Dicey Field show — another few hundred people out.

Kerosene Record Company

Around Retrograde I'd ended up building an informal business just to keep the band running — photographers, marketing, people rigging and building our stages, all of it volunteer. Nobody was getting paid, and that bothered me. After the band ended, I started Kerosene Record Company with Matthias Loweski to try to actually pay the artists and crew who'd been doing that work for free, and we began planning a festival called Magpie. Contract issues kept the festival from happening, but we ran as an operating S corp for about a year.

Rob Wolf & Willy on the Wire

I got hired as Rob Wolf's keyboard player in March 2026, and joined Willy on the Wire shortly after. The two acts share a core group of musicians. Since then we've been the inaugural band for the Bandwagon Mobile Stage, opened for Eli Young as part of Rob Wolf's lineup, and played the MoJan Festival. Willy on the Wire is booked for the CP Festival in summer 2027.
